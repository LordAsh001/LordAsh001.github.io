      How to spot a dumpsite on Sentinel-2

      At 10 m per pixel, a site shows clearly once it covers about 0.5 ha (70 × 70 m). Look for:

      • a patchy, speckled surface in grey, brown and white with no tidy structure;
      • bare ground with a ring of access tracks where trucks drive in and out;
      • dark smoke or burn scars, often next to a river, a flood plain, or a quarry or borrow pit;
      • growth over time: compare the 2023 cloudless mosaic with the latest image in Copernicus Browser.

      Confirm with the sharper Esri imagery before you pin a site. The best evidence is a field visit or a local report.

      Method and sources
      1. Basemap: Sentinel-2 cloudless 2023 by EOX (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Each popup links to the latest Sentinel-2 L2A scene in Copernicus Browser.
      2. Documented sites: peer-reviewed studies and official sources (cited in each popup). Green means the source gives the coordinates; amber means the source gives only a study-area box or a site extent, so the marker is placed at its centre.
      3. Newly confirmed: candidates from a Sentinel-2 Random Forest scan in Google Earth Engine and reports sent in through a public KoboToolbox form. Each one is checked by a person in QGIS before it is published here.
      4. GPS field survey: 57 disposal sites in Nsukka Urban, each recorded on the ground with a GPS waypoint app and classed as authorised or unauthorised (Isimah et al. 2026, Research Square preprint, CC BY 4.0).
      5. OpenStreetMap: features tagged landuse=landfill or amenity=waste_transfer_station, fetched live from the Overpass API (ODbL).
      6. GRID3 Nigeria: dump-site points from the eHealth Africa field survey (2017–18), fetched live from the GRID3 GeoServer. These may include sites that have since closed.
      7. Status: "open" means the site is reported as operating or visibly active in recent imagery. Status changes, so check the date on each source.
